Expander Graphs and Graph Neural Networks: Predicting Spectral Gap Properties

December 2024 Graph Theory Machine Learning Research

In this post, we explore the fascinating world of expander graphs, their crucial role in computer science and mathematics, and our novel approach to predicting their spectral properties using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs).

What are Expander Graphs?

Expander graphs are sparse graphs that exhibit excellent connectivity properties. Formally, an expander graph is a graph where every subset of vertices has a large boundary relative to its size. This means that removing any small set of vertices or edges doesn't disconnect the graph into many small components.

More precisely, a graph \(G = (V, E)\) is called an \((\alpha, \beta)\)-expander if for every subset \(S \subseteq V\) with \(|S| \leq \alpha|V|\), the number of edges between \(S\) and \(V \setminus S\) is at least \(\beta|S|\). Here, \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are constants that determine the expansion quality.

Expander Graphs and Spectral Methods

Expander graphs are sparse graphs that exhibit excellent connectivity properties, making them fundamental in network design, distributed computing, and algorithm design. These graphs maintain robust connectivity even when some nodes or edges fail, providing reliable communication patterns and efficient routing structures. The key insight is that expander graphs have a large boundary relative to the size of any subset of vertices, ensuring that information flows efficiently throughout the network.

The most powerful method for identifying and analyzing expander graphs is through spectral methods, which examine the eigenvalues of the graph's adjacency or Laplacian matrix. The spectral gap—the difference between the largest and second-largest eigenvalues—serves as a crucial measure of expansion quality. Ramanujan graphs represent the optimal case, achieving the theoretical bound of \(2\sqrt{d-1}\) for \(d\)-regular graphs, where \(d\) is the degree. For the normalized Laplacian matrix, the spectral gap ranges from 0 to 2, with values close to 2 indicating excellent expansion properties and values close to 0 indicating poor connectivity. This spectral characterization provides both theoretical insights and practical tools for understanding graph structure and performance.

Our GNN Model for Spectral Gap Prediction

We train graph neural networks on 50,000 synthetic graphs that include different families of graphs such as Erdős-Rényi graphs, Cycle, Cliques, Stochastic block models, Watts-Strogatz (small world models), 2D grid, and many others. We train this model to predict the spectral gap.

Our GNN model includes a graph regression problem that includes message passing layers, graph convolutional layers, and then a sort convolution layer. For more details, our paper will be out soon.
